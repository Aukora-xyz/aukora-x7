Each row accounts for one old named law. S describes what an outcome must preserve. A describes a promised success. V records a version dependency in either a conclusion or its domain. A guarded statement can omit an entire new class without contradicting the old statement. A closed conclusion can exclude a new class even when its operation is safe.

## Scope of the bridge

A trace restricted to 01 starts with a 01 history and submits 01 frames. Feed, reset and sidecars retain their separate port semantics. The trace lemmas compare the same candidate, initial state and input. Its individual verdicts are shared by these two projections. The separate verdict laws compare admission against the installed policy, evidence and halt conditions. The historical evidence lemmas also apply to any prior record whose installed interpretation is unchanged. Restricting only the final frame while allowing the prior record to contain new evidence does not give the old parser a meaning for that evidence. The two-reader comparison therefore records that hypothesis explicitly.

The installed evidence reader preserves the committed raw bytes for positions, heads and bundle digests. An extension can expose canonical evidence fields without replacing the bytes in the record. The table-growth theorem fixes old projections, complete typed wire results, rule/risk/finding effects, sidecars and historical evidence lookup. Each later slot can add representations for the operation and state types in this executable. A new public state type or executable handler requires a separate mechanism.

The frozen L8 checker asks whether the submitted full machine satisfies `Aukora.Release.Lawful`. Its old record law rejects an accepted nonlegacy control on that machine. The new satisfaction and transition certificates do not establish this false proposition. The format-01 install carrier and unhalted state do not change its type. The grant, staging, file/package, expenditure and equality-under-the-hash-hypothesis obligations survive.
